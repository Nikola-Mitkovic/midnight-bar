Spades and hearts have researched backs. See their source guides. Flavor and pairing notes are editorial suggestions.

## A ♠ Old Fashioned

- 45 ml (1.5 oz) bourbon or rye whiskey
- 1 (1) sugar cube
- A few dashes (A few dashes) Angostura bitters
- A few dashes (A few dashes) water
- To garnish (To garnish) orange zest and cocktail cherry

Dissolve sugar with bitters and water. Add ice and whiskey; stir and garnish.

One thing to learn: Expressing citrus means bending the peel, coloured side toward the drink, to spray aromatic oils onto its surface.

What to expect: A slow, whiskey-led sip: oak, caramel, orange oil, and baking spice, with sweetness rounding the edges. Try alongside toasted pecans.

## 2 ♠ Manhattan

- 50 ml (1.67 oz) rye whiskey
- 20 ml (0.67 oz) sweet red vermouth
- 1 dash (1 dash) Angostura bitters
- 1 (1) cocktail cherry

Stir whiskey, vermouth, and bitters with ice; strain into a chilled coupe and add a cherry.

One thing to learn: Stirring chills and dilutes without the aeration of shaking, keeping this spirit-led drink clear and silky.

What to expect: Rye spice meets dark fruit and herbal sweetness, with a gently bitter finish. A small piece of aged cheddar makes a savory contrast.

## 3 ♠ Whiskey Sour

- 45 ml (1.5 oz) bourbon whiskey
- 25 ml (0.83 oz) fresh lemon juice
- 20 ml (0.67 oz) simple syrup
- A few drops, optional (A few drops, optional) pasteurized egg white
- To garnish (To garnish) half orange slice and cocktail cherry

Shake bourbon, lemon, syrup, and optional egg white with ice. Strain over fresh ice; garnish.

One thing to learn: Egg white adds foam and a softer texture rather than sweetness. The drink also works without it.

What to expect: Bright lemon first, then rounded bourbon and a sweet-tart finish. With egg white it feels softer and silkier; without, more direct and crisp.

## 4 ♠ Mint Julep

- 60 ml (2 oz) bourbon whiskey
- 4 sprigs (4 sprigs) fresh mint
- 1 tsp (1 tsp) powdered sugar
- 2 tsp (2 tsp) water
- To fill (To fill) cracked ice
- 1 sprig (1 sprig) mint for garnish

Gently press mint with sugar and water. Add cracked ice and bourbon; stir until frosty.

One thing to learn: Cracked ice packs closely around the drink and chills it quickly. Top it up after stirring as the first ice settles.

What to expect: Cold mint aroma over gently sweet bourbon. It is refreshing but still whiskey-forward, without citrus tartness. Try with smoky grilled food.

## 5 ♠ Sazerac

- 7.5 ml (0.25 oz) Herbsaint, for rinsing (discard excess)
- To garnish (To garnish) lemon peel

Stir rye, sugar, and bitters with ice. Strain into a chilled Herbsaint-rinsed glass; add lemon peel.

What to expect: Concentrated rye spice, lemon oil, and a distinctive anise aroma. It is a short, lightly sweet sipping drink with no citrus juice.

## 6 ♠ Boulevardier

## 7 ♠ Irish Coffee

- 50 ml (1.67 oz) Irish whiskey
- 120 ml (4 oz) hot black coffee
- 50 ml (1.67 oz) fresh cream, chilled
- 1 tsp (1 tsp) sugar

Stir whiskey and sugar into hot coffee in a warmed glass. Float chilled cream over a spoon.

One thing to learn: A cream float needs a slow pour close to the surface. Pourable, slightly thick cream is easier to layer than stiff whipped cream.

What to expect: Hot, lightly sweet coffee under cool, rich cream, with mellow whiskey warmth. A little shortbread suits its coffee-and-cream character.

## 8 ♠ Rusty Nail

- To garnish (To garnish) lemon zest

Pour Scotch and Drambuie over ice, stir gently, and finish with lemon zest.

## 9 ♠ Penicillin

- 60 ml (2 oz) blended Scotch whisky

- 22.5 ml (0.75 oz) honey syrup
- 2–3 coin-size slices (2–3 coin-size slices) fresh ginger

What to expect: Lemon brightness and honey give way to fresh ginger heat. The smoky top layer reaches your nose first, rather than dominating every sip.

## 10 ♠ Rob Roy

- 60 ml (2 oz) blended Scotch whisky
- 30 ml (1 oz) sweet vermouth
- 2 dashes (2 dashes) Angostura bitters
- 1 (1) cocktail cherry

Stir Scotch, vermouth, and bitters over ice; strain into a chilled coupe and garnish with cherry.

One thing to learn: Changing the base spirit can define a new cocktail: a Rob Roy follows the Manhattan family but uses Scotch rather than rye or bourbon.

What to expect: Malt, dark fruit, herbs, and gentle sweetness in a silky sip. Smoke depends on the Scotch you choose; it is not inevitable.

## J ♠ New York Sour

- 60 ml (2 oz) rye whiskey or bourbon
- 30 ml (1 oz) fresh lemon juice
- 22.5 ml (0.75 oz) simple syrup
- A few drops, optional (A few drops, optional) pasteurized egg white
- 15 ml (0.5 oz) dry red wine, such as Shiraz or Malbec
- To garnish (To garnish) lemon zest and cocktail cherry

Shake whiskey, lemon, syrup, and optional egg white. Strain over ice, then gently float red wine.

One thing to learn: Floating means gently layering one liquid over another. Keep the spoon close to the surface and pour slowly to avoid disturbing the layer.

What to expect: A bright whiskey sour with a fruity, slightly tannic red-wine finish. The wine adds depth and a drier edge rather than extra sweetness.

## Q ♠ Godfather
